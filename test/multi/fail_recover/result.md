## Takeaways

- **Safety held everywhere:** 0 acked writes lost across crashes, a full-cluster crash under load, partitions, flapping and a rolling restart. Every node converged to the same data.
- **Failover ≈ 200-500 ms** (median 227 ms), consistent with the 150-300 ms election timeout. The worst stalls under load were ~690 ms (flapping, and the leader's turn in the rolling restart).
- **The minority leader can't commit, but it doesn't step down either.** Its write hung for the full 5 s propose timeout and then returned Error, and its uncommitted `v2` was correctly overwritten by `v3` within ~110 ms of healing. Adding a check-quorum step-down would make it fail fast.
- **Stale reads on the minority side:** the cut-off old leader kept answering `q = v1` after the majority had committed `v3`, because `server.read.from=any`. Use `leader` reads (plus a check-quorum or ReadIndex step) if reads must be linearizable.
- **The heal causes a stall:** about 340 ms with no writes right after a 2 | 3 split heals. The cut-off followers kept bumping their term, and with no PreVote they force a fresh election on rejoin, even though the same node wins it again.
- **Catch-up is slow, about 210 entries/s.** A follower `fsync`s every entry on its own (`raft/replicate.go` append loop, one `persistAppendLocked` per entry), so a 64-entry batch takes ~300 ms. That's longer than the 80 ms RPC attempt timeout, so the leader retries every batch. A node that is 1M entries behind would need ~80 min. One `fsync` per batch would fix it. A wiped disk catches up at the same speed as an intact WAL, because the time goes into the follower's appends, not into walking `nextIndex` back.
- **A full-cluster restart is quick:** ~600 ms to a leader, then <100 ms for every node to re-apply from its WAL.
