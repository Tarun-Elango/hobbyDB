hobbyDB: a Raft KV store in Go with higher write throughput and lower p99 than etcd on a 
3-node cluster. Same durability (fsync before ack), linearizable reads, checked under crashes
and partitions.

--------------------------------

1	Group commit and pipelined AppendEntries. One fsync per batch, more than one RPC in flight.	Writes don’t scale with clients (197 → 212/s on a single node from 1 to 64 clients). Batching should take you from ~100/s into the thousands. (Opportunistic drain (zero extra wait): While one persistAppendBatchLocked + fsync is running, incoming Propose calls queue up. As soon as it finishes, drain up to maxBatchSize (e.g., 64 or 128) queued entries and fsync them all together in one call.)
2	ReadIndex or leader lease.	The stalled-leader linearizability test currently FAILs. You can’t claim etcd’s guarantees until it passes.
3	Check-quorum step-down.	A leader cut off from the majority hangs writes for 5 s. It should fail fast.
4	Snapshots and log compaction.	A 1M-record WAL means 1.1 GiB RSS and a 2 s restart. Without snapshots, long benchmark runs aren’t credible.
5	etcd head-to-head. Same machine, 3 nodes, 1–256 clients, 128 B / 4 KiB / 64 KiB values. Report writes/s and p50/p99/p999.	This is the result you publish.
6	Add explicit transition methods for election and update flow diagrams accordingly.	Centralizes Raft role changes (Follower/Candidate/Leader) so side effects like applyCond.Broadcast() and election-timer resets can't be missed, and keeps flow/ docs in sync.
7.  check/add settings to tweak consitency / availability, and performance
